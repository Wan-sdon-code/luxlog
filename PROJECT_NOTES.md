# LuxLog — My Notes

## What is LuxLog?

Sometimes a customer gets equipment or a service for free.

The customer pays **$0**, but the business still spends money.

LuxLog shows how much that free setup may actually cost the business.

---

## What does my Python do?

It is very simple:

1. Check how many hours the equipment was used.
2. Work out how much the equipment use costs.
3. Check how many hours the staff worked.
4. Work out how much the staff time costs.
5. Add both costs together.
6. Take away what the customer paid.
7. Show how much the business has to pay.
8. Give a simple warning: **OK, CHECK or TOO MUCH.**

---

## Simple Example

Equipment used for **8 hours**

`8 × $30 = $240`

Staff worked for **5 hours**

`5 × $20 = $100`

Total cost:

`$240 + $100 = $340`

Customer paid:

**$0**

So the business covers:

**$340**

Result:

🟠 **CHECK**

---

## Why $200 and $500?

I chose these numbers as examples.

They are **not real company limits**.

If this was used by a real company, I would use limits that make sense for that business.

---

## Why did I use Python?

Instead of calculating everything myself every time, Python does the calculation for me.

I can change the hours or costs and run it again.

---

## What did I learn?

Something can be free for the customer but still cost the business money.

The important thing is knowing **how much it costs** before deciding whether giving it away is worth it.

---

## If Someone Asks Me What LuxLog Is

> **"LuxLog shows how much a free customer setup may actually cost the business after equipment and staff costs are included."**
